# The level ladder, in depth

Use this before you score anything. The point of the ladder is not to grade
harshly; it is to make the next point obvious. Every level below 9 has one
dominant reason it is stuck, and naming that reason is most of the fix.

---

## The anchor set (describe it to yourself before scoring)

The clearest 9-level reference this skill was built against is a strip of
eight iPhone screens, each a different app, laid side by side. Read it as a
lesson, screen by screen, because the difference between its weakest and its
strongest screen is the whole ladder in one image.

1. **Dark finance dashboard (the 6–7 in the set).** Near-black ground, "Good
   morning, Maya Lindqvist" with an avatar, `€4,382.16` with small decimals,
   a lime "€214 less spent than last week" pill, a bar chart card, three
   category tiles (Groceries, Eating out, Transport), a lime tab bar. It is
   competent, and it is the category default: greeting header, hero stat card,
   tiles. It is the screen in the strip you forget first. *Lesson: polish does
   not lift a default structure past 7.*
2. **"€137.60 left in the jar until Sunday, about €45 a day."** Off-white
   ground, a large drawn glass jar holding copper coins filled to exactly the
   remaining amount, a ruler up the jar's side (Full €450.00, Mon −61.30,
   Tue −88.35…), then a "Today" list (Marlow Grocers −€42.80). *Lesson: the
   picture holds the data (P1). Hide every number and you still know how much
   is left.*
3. **Red "LONG RUN 6.8 KM".** Saturated red ground, a huge condensed `6.8`
   with a small `KM`, a track oval drawn in concentric lines, three stats
   (`5:52 /km`, `39:46`, `512`), one white "Start" pill, a red tab bar.
   *Lesson: one display moment, small units, a drawing that is the activity
   itself, one action.*
4. **Night house (22:41).** A drawn house under a crescent moon, lit windows,
   chimney smoke; below it an evening routine with times: "Kitchen closed
   21:58", "Warm shower, not hot 22:20", "Tomorrow's three things · Now"
   (highlighted), "Twenty pages, paper book 22:45", "Lights out, yours 23:05";
   a mini player "Rain on a tin roof · Fades out at 23:19"; an alarm chip "Fri
   6:50". *Lesson: moment-awareness (P7) and copy you would quote (P6). The
   house's lights are the routine's progress.*
5. **Chartreuse "Big Monstera".** A giant dark-green leaf bleeding off the top
   right, the plant's name in heavy condensed type, three stats (`650 ml
   every 8 days`, `Sat 11 next water`, `1,850 lux, 28 Sep`), a strip of leaf
   photos, "Watered" pill plus "Skip tonight". *Lesson: coverage (P2) and bleed;
   the subject is the hero, not an icon of it.*
6. **Mustard "Al banco".** Serif italic title, "5 of 8 said · Lesson 34 ·
   Coppe", a drawn Italian playing card (six of cups) with "Quanto le devo?
   How much do I owe you?", cards fanned behind it, a speaker button and a
   "Hold and say it" pill with a red dot. *Lesson: a concept-born signature
   interaction (P8) and a hero object from the culture of the subject.*
7. **Blue "16 KM", "Sat 3 Oct · week 9 of 15".** A red vertical progress
   ruler running down the screen edge (where you are in the plan), `16` huge
   with small `KM`, "Easy at 5:50 to 6:05. Last 3 km at race pace, 5:05.", a
   week strip M–S with distances (`8.2 10 6 – 16 7`), a "START" pill. *Lesson:
   data as an object even without illustration: the ruler is the plan.*
8. **Blue "Big Monstera".** The same plant app in another direction: a
   chartreuse leaf in a white pot on cobalt, the human line "650 ml, slowly,
   until it runs from the drainage hole. Living room, east window.",
   "Watered" + "Skip tonight", a tab bar with an active pill. *Lesson: a
   direction is a whole world, not a recolour.*

What the 9s share (the nine pillars, observed): a status bar whose time fits
the content; a small meta line with a round icon button at top right; one hero
(picture or giant number) that encodes state; a row of three stats; one
primary pill plus one text action; a tab bar tinted to the ground; one
drawing hand per app in the ground's own tones; type with one display moment
and small units; copy with place and sense; no dead space anywhere.

**The second anchor (7.5):** a Shinkansen "My Journeys" app. Pale grey,
a lovely illustration (train, Fuji, cherry blossom, torii), segmented tabs,
a white card with `09:00 → 11:12`, a black "Show ticket" button, a journey
details list, a floating tab bar. Very polished and very real. Capped at 7.5
because the illustration decorates (it is the same picture whatever happens),
every field has the same weight, and the structure is every travel app's.

**The calibration screenshots** in `examples/` are one project at three levels:
- `r1-*` (5.5–6.5): strong concept, type on a ground, empty middles, no chrome,
  a broken primary button. The anti-slop plateau.
- `r2-*` (8–8.5): hero objects that carry state, full skeleton, human copy.
- `r3-*` (8.5–9): signature heroes per direction, a detail pass, defects fixed.

Open them side by side before your first score.

## Reading any reference: the gallery lesson

When the user shares a gallery of "best of iOS" screens, score each one before
borrowing from it. Galleries mix 9s with 6s, and copying a 6 is how you stay at 6.
Observed in one such gallery:

| Screen | Level | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A holographic debit card tilting at an angle on white, "Your future card has arrived. And it's holographic.", two stacked pills | 9 | The artifact *is* the screen; material + motion; one line of copy you would quote |
| A run turned into a till receipt (TOTAL 7.49 KM, AVG PACE, "*** DUPLICATE COPY ***") | 9 | Artifact hero mode; the format itself is the joke and the data |
| A voice recorder with a speaker grille, a cyan level meter, hardware keys (RESTART / PAUSE / STOP) | 9 | Instrument hero; physical controls with pressed states |
| "$400" in a gold serif glow on a dark star field, amount chips, "From: Checking 0578" | 8.5–9 | Number + atmosphere; restraint |
| A breathing black liquid orb for an assistant, two suggestion chips, composer | 8.5 | Material presence that encodes idle/thinking/speaking |
| Glass rings to pick an appearance, rendered as real glass | 8.5 | Material hero for personalisation |
| A radial line chart of a fund's holdings with one segment highlighted | 8.5 | Chart-as-art |
| A tick-ruler slider for relay distance, runners' avatars, three stat tiles | 7 | Instrument input is good; **rocket emoji** in a card and a generic red gradient cap it |
| Onboarding with emoji inline in the headline, floating tag pills, a blue "Continue" | 5–6 | Emoji in copy, default blue, a metaphor that needs reading |
| A paywall with two book covers, purple "Yearly / Monthly" toggles, purple Subscribe | 6 | Purple default, no product working on screen |
| A zodiac list in serif on grey with chevrons | 7 | Typographic taste, but a plain list: P1 and P2 at 0 by design |
| A plain profile form ("Personalise Profile") | 6.5 | Competent native form; fine for its job, not a hero screen |

Take from the 9s their *mode* (artifact, instrument, material), never their
pixels. Name the cap on the weaker ones out loud so you do not inherit it.

## Each level, with its tells and its exit

### Level 3 — Template
**Tells:** system blue, default component kit, emoji in buttons or headings,
purple-to-blue gradient hero, glass cards, "Welcome back!", lorem or "John Doe",
icons from three different families.
**Exit:** decide the ground, type, accent and content yourself. Remove every
default you did not choose.

### Level 4 — Competent generic
**Tells:** greeting header with avatar; hero stat card; 2×2 tile grid;
"Recent activity" list; Inter or SF at three sizes; one safe accent; equal
padding everywhere; every element in a card.
**Why it is stuck:** each choice is the most likely one. Defensible
individually, average together.
**Exit:** Step 3 (moments). The moment's one fact replaces the dashboard.

### Level 5 — Polished generic
**Tells:** real-looking data, OK hierarchy, perhaps a nice gradient or a stock
illustration in the header, decent spacing. All values look equally important.
It could ship, and nobody would remember it.
**Exit:** a direction with a reason (Step 5) and one display moment (P5).

### Level 6 — The anti-slop plateau (the most common "good AI" result)
**Tells:** clichés gone; a distinctive palette and a real type choice; huge
numbers; but the screen is typography on a colour field. The middle third is
empty. No status bar, no tab bar, no primary pill: a poster in a phone. Any
illustration is small, decorative, or line-art too thin to carry weight.
Usually one real defect (a clipped word, a button that renders as text).
**Why it is stuck:** it removed the bad without adding the rich. Taste by
subtraction.
**Exit:** P1 + P2 + P4. Draw the hero object; make it fill the middle; wrap
it in real chrome. This single move was worth +2 to +2.5 in the case study.

### Level 7 — The convention plateau
**Tells:** looks like a real, shipped, well-made app. Proper chrome, a pleasant
illustration, clean cards, clear CTA. And it looks like the category leader.
**Why it is stuck:** it studied the best apps and arrived where they are. The
illustration decorates instead of informing.
**Exit:** keep the grammar, refuse the picture (Step 2). Make the illustration
change with the data (P1) and the screen change with the moment (P7).

### Level 8 — Distinctive and complete
**Tells:** a real idea, hero objects that mostly carry state, full skeleton,
consistent world, good copy. But the drawings are clean primitives without
character, the directions share one structure, and something small is off (a
crowded stat, a toast over a label, an orphaned label).
**Why it is stuck:** the last 10% is detail, character and proof.
**Exit:** P3 + P8 + P9. A detail pass on every scene, a unique signature hero
per direction, a full audit, and fixing every last defect.

### Level 9 — Every pillar
**Tells:** you can read the state from the picture alone; every screen is the
same world; there is one moment people would show a friend; every line of copy
is specific; at 3× nothing is clipped, crowded or misaligned; it has been
rendered and inspected.
**What it still lacks for 10:** art with a human hand's texture and
imperfection, motion tuned on hardware, and an idea that changes the category.

## How to score (the procedure)

1. Render every state. Open each PNG at full size. Run the audit.
2. Score P1–P9 (0 / 0.5 / 1), one line of evidence each. Half points are for
   "present but weak" (a hero that carries state on one screen of five; a
   detail pass on some scenes).
3. Score = 1 + sum. Apply the caps (SKILL.md §2).
4. Place your sheet next to an anchor and write one sentence on where it
   loses. If you cannot name a loss, you have not looked hard enough.
5. Write the top three blockers, ranked by points gained per hour.

## Common misjudgments

| You think | Reality |
|---|---|
| "It's clean and has a nice palette, so 8" | Clean + palette is 5–6. 8 needs a hero that carries state inside real chrome |
| "The type is huge and bold, so it's premium" | Huge type over an empty middle is the L6 plateau |
| "I added an illustration" | Does it change when the data changes? If not, it is decoration (P1 = 0) |
| "Four directions, very different" | Same layout and same scenes with new colours are skins (cap 8) |
| "The code looks right" | Unrendered = cap 7. Real bugs hid in code that looked right (audit.md) |
| "Minimal is premium" | Minimal without a hero and without detail reads empty, not expensive |
| "It matches the top app in the category" | That is the definition of 7 |
| "One tiny clipped label doesn't matter" | One FAIL caps at 6. Users see it first |
